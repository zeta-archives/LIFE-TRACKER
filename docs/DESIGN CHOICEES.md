# 1. PROJECT SCOPE AND DESIGN VISION

## 1.1 Platform Decision — LOCKED

Life Tracker will be developed as a **desktop web application intended primarily for use on Windows computers**.

The product will run in a web browser. We are not developing an Android application or a separate native Windows application as part of the current scope.

All design decisions should prioritize the desktop web experience, including large-screen layouts, detailed dashboards, interactive visualizations, comprehensive navigation, and efficient keyboard and mouse interactions.

The interface should accommodate reasonable variations in desktop browser-window size without sacrificing its intended visual composition.

Do not spend development time designing separate mobile navigation systems or mobile-specific experiences.

## 1.2 Core Design Vision

Life Tracker should feel like a personal sanctuary for navigating the chaos of life.

It is not simply a productivity dashboard, task manager, or time-tracking application. Its visual experience should reflect its purpose: helping users understand their lives, pursue their ambitions, manage responsibilities, and maintain awareness of what matters to them.

The interface should combine cinematic visual design, intelligent interactions, interconnected life areas, meaningful data visualization, and practical tools for everyday planning.

The application should feel like one cohesive environment rather than a collection of unrelated utilities.

## 1.3 Core Personality

Life Tracker should feel:

* **Warm:** A welcoming personal environment that feels like home.
* **Intelligent:** A system that helps users understand and navigate life's complexity.
* **Cinematic:** Visually atmospheric, sophisticated, and carefully art-directed.
* **Magical:** Subtle wonder and distinctive visual details that make the experience memorable.
* **Personal:** A reflection of the user's ambitions, personality, priorities, and way of life.

These qualities must coexist with clarity, accessibility, practicality, and reliable functionality.

The goal is an interface with a distinctive identity, not a visually elaborate application that is difficult to use.

## 1.4 What Life Tracker Must Not Feel Like

Avoid interfaces that feel:

* Corporate or sterile.
* Childish or excessively playful.
* Like another generic productivity dashboard or time-tracking application.
* Like a generic AI-generated interface assembled from unrelated components.
* Overloaded with decorative effects or unnecessary gamification.
* Visually impressive but confusing or impractical.

Every major design decision should contribute to a coherent visual identity or improve the user's experience.

# 3. THE SOUL ORB

## 3.1 Concept

The Soul Orb is a proposed signature visual element of Life Tracker.

It should feel like a sophisticated, luminous object representing the application's intelligence and its role in connecting different aspects of the user's life.

It may become one of the most recognizable elements of the product.

The concept is established, but its final appearance remains undecided.

## 3.2 Visual Direction

Explore concepts involving:

* A luminous sphere or abstract energy form.
* Rich light and shadow.
* Subtle internal depth.
* Carefully controlled highlights.
* A distinctive silhouette.
* A sense of life without a cartoon-like personality.
* Restrained, purposeful movement.

The orb could be dimensional, glass-like, atmospheric, or abstract. These possibilities should be explored before selecting an implementation.

Avoid generic glowing spheres, excessive neon effects, and decorative particle systems without a clear purpose.

## 3.3 Three Roles

The Soul Orb may serve three distinct purposes.

**Visual identity:** Anchor the Life Orbit experience and contribute to Life Tracker's recognizable appearance.

**Interaction:** Provide an entry point to AI-assisted interactions or respond to meaningful user selections.

**Contextual feedback:** Reflect clearly defined application states, such as the availability of a new recommendation.

The orb must not pretend to understand the user's emotions or indicate an application state that the underlying system cannot determine.

## 3.4 Placement and Animation

Potential placements include:

* The Life Orbit home experience.
* The AI assistant.
* Selected Life Sync interactions.
* Meaningful milestones.

The orb should not become an oversized animated object on every screen.

Its ambient movement should be subtle. More expressive animation can be reserved for meaningful interactions.

The precise appearance, lighting, materials, color, placement, and behavior remain open for visual exploration.

# 5. SHAPE LANGUAGE AND VISUAL HIERARCHY — LOCKED PRINCIPLES

## 5.1 Layout and Surfaces

Do not place every element inside a rounded card.

Use a deliberate combination of:

* Open layouts.
* Structured panels.
* Dividers.
* Subtle surface variations.
* Cards where they provide meaningful grouping.
* Timelines and structured lists.
* Distinctive spatial compositions where appropriate.

Cards are useful when they establish a meaningful grouping or interactive boundary. They should not become the default container for every piece of information.

## 5.2 Visual Hierarchy

The interface must communicate which information deserves attention.

Important deadlines, required actions, scheduling conflicts, and meaningful recommendations should stand out from secondary information.

Decorative details and minor statistics must not compete with important tasks.

Each screen should have a clear primary purpose and a hierarchy suited to that purpose.

## 5.3 Shape Consistency

Maintain consistent spacing, borders, corner radii, and surface treatments.

Initial corner-radius values may be explored around:

* 8 px for compact controls.
* 12 px for ordinary components.
* 18 px for larger panels.

These are starting values, not mandatory sizes for every element. The final scale should be determined through actual interface design.

## 5.4 Visual Effects

Gradients, shadows, glows, translucent surfaces, and similar effects are allowed when they support the product's identity or communicate information.

Do not apply them indiscriminately.

The cinematic appearance should come from composition, hierarchy, lighting, and restraint, not from applying the same glow to every component.

# 6. INTERACTION AND MOTION — LOCKED PRINCIPLES

Motion should improve the experience rather than distract from it.

## 6.1 Purposeful Animation

Animations may:

* Communicate a state change.
* Provide interaction feedback.
* Reveal additional information.
* Explain relationships.
* Clarify navigation.
* Reinforce the Soul Orb's role.

Ordinary buttons, tables, forms, and task lists do not need elaborate animations.

## 6.2 Timing

Initial timing guidelines:

* Small interactions: approximately 150–220 milliseconds.
* Larger transitions: approximately 220–360 milliseconds.

These are starting points to refine during testing.

## 6.3 Accessibility and Performance

* Respect reduced-motion preferences.
* Do not require animation to access essential information.
* Avoid motion that interferes with reading or data entry.
* Preserve responsive interactions.
* Use complex rendering only where it produces meaningful value.

## 6.4 Desktop Interaction

The application should prioritize the desktop browser experience.

Design for mouse and keyboard interaction, with clear hover, focus, selected, active, loading, and error states where applicable.

Keyboard navigation should be considered for common actions and interactive elements.

The layout should remain usable when the browser window is resized.

# 7. DATA VISUALIZATION — LOCKED PRINCIPLES

Every visualization must help the user answer a meaningful question.

## 7.1 Attendance

Help users understand their current attendance percentage, attendance history, and how future absences or attended classes could change the percentage.

## 7.2 Time Allocation

Help users understand where their recorded time went and how it compares with their intentions.

## 7.3 Goal Progress

Distinguish completed milestones, remaining work, relevant targets, and deadlines.

## 7.4 Sleep Trends

Present sleep duration and schedule consistency over meaningful periods.

## 7.5 Planned Versus Actual

Reveal relevant differences between planned activities and recorded behavior without automatically judging the user.

## 7.6 Life Map

Show meaningful relationships among goals, milestones, projects, routines, tasks, and activities.

## 7.7 Visualization Rules

* Do not use circular progress indicators for every metric.
* Select visualizations according to their purpose.
* Use accurate data and clearly defined calculations.
* Never invent statistics.
* Do not create a universal score purporting to measure how successfully someone is living.
* Apply color consistently.
* Never use color as the only means of communicating critical information.
* Prioritize readable labels, units, useful comparisons, and sufficient contrast.
* Use filtering and progressive disclosure when complexity increases.
* Present correlations as observations rather than established causes when the data cannot establish causation.

# 9. CORE ANTI-AI-SLOP STANDARD

Every significant visual decision must have a reason.

Avoid defaulting to familiar interface patterns simply because they are easy for AI tools to generate.

## 9.1 Patterns to Challenge

* Repetitive card grids.
* Identical layouts across unrelated screens.
* Unnecessary purple-to-blue gradients.
* Excessive neon effects and glassmorphism.
* Decorative glowing borders around everything.
* Generic typography without a considered hierarchy.
* Unnecessary rounded containers.
* Charts that look impressive but communicate little.
* Excessive animations.
* Decorative statistics without meaningful information.
* Components copied from unrelated libraries without adaptation.
* Interfaces that look impressive in screenshots but fail during real interaction.

These elements are not universally forbidden. They must have a deliberate purpose when used.

## 9.2 Quality Standard

Evaluate the interface using:

* Actual screenshots.
* Realistic content.
* Functional interactions.
* Different desktop browser-window sizes.
* Empty and populated states.
* Error and loading states.
* Keyboard and mouse interaction.
* Accessibility checks.
* Performance testing.

A screen is not finished simply because an AI coding tool has generated working code.

The objective is an intentional, coherent interface with recognizable personality and practical usability.

# 11. LIFE ORBIT — PRIMARY HOME-SCREEN CONCEPT

## 11.1 Core Concept

**Life Orbit is the primary visual concept for the Life Tracker homepage.**

It develops the user's circular 3D-carousel idea into an interactive constellation organized around the Soul Orb.

The objective is to communicate that the user's life areas are distinct but interconnected parts of one overall system.

Life Orbit should become one of Life Tracker's recognizable visual experiences.

## 11.2 Overall Composition

The proposed composition includes:

* A central Soul Orb.
* Life-area nodes arranged around it.
* Subtle visual connections between relevant areas.
* A sense of spatial depth.
* Coordinated but distinguishable accent colors.
* Interactive selection of individual life areas.
* Contextual indicators for relevant progress or concerns.

The nodes should remain easy to understand and select.

The design should communicate the overall structure without requiring users to rotate through every section sequentially.

## 11.3 Why a Constellation Rather Than a Traditional Carousel?

A traditional carousel can hide content and force users to navigate through items sequentially.

Life Orbit should make the main life areas understandable at a glance.

An interactive constellation allows users to see multiple areas simultaneously, select the area they want, and understand that these areas belong to one connected system.

The exact layout, degree of dimensional depth, node arrangement, and animation remain open to refinement.

## 11.4 Selecting a Life Area

Selecting a node should bring the relevant area into focus.

For example, selecting Academics could reveal a compact preview containing:

* Attendance summary.
* Upcoming assignments.
* Current academic goals.
* Relevant progress information.
* Important upcoming concerns where supported by actual data.

Opening the selected area should navigate to its full workspace.

Different areas should display information appropriate to their purpose.

## 11.5 Performance

Life Orbit may use a spatial, slightly three-dimensional composition if the chosen implementation supports it reliably.

Complex effects must not compromise responsiveness, readability, or ordinary interactions.

Start with a carefully composed interactive representation. Add deeper 3D effects and richer animation only where they improve the experience.

## 11.6 Relationship With Life Map

Life Orbit and Life Map serve different purposes.

**Life Orbit** provides a high-level view of the user's life areas.

**Life Map** provides a detailed visualization of relationships among goals, milestones, projects, tasks, routines, and connected records.

Life Orbit answers:

"What are the major areas of my life, and where should I go?"

Life Map answers:

"How are my goals and activities connected?"

They must complement rather than duplicate one another.

# 14. UNIVERSAL QUICK LOG SYSTEM

## 14.1 Purpose

Quick Log is the common mechanism for recording everyday activities across the application.

Users should not have to navigate through a complicated series of screens to record routine information.

A quick action should open the smallest appropriate interaction for the requested activity.

## 14.2 Three Universal Actions

Make the following actions easily accessible throughout Life Tracker.

### Log

Record something that happened.

Examples:

* Mark a class as attended.
* Record a study session.
* Log a workout.
* Record sleep and wake times.
* Record creative practice.
* Add a progress measurement.

### Plan

Schedule something or create an actionable commitment.

Examples:

* Create a task.
* Schedule a study session.
* Add a workout.
* Schedule a personal commitment.
* Create a recurring routine.
* Add an event to the calendar.

### Ask

Use the AI assistant to understand or organize personal information.

Examples:

* Ask about progress.
* Request a schedule adjustment.
* Understand a conflict.
* Review a goal.
* Record supported activities using natural-language instructions.

The assistant should only modify information through supported operations and must preserve user control over consequential changes.

## 14.3 Context-Sensitive Logging

The available actions should depend on the activity and the context where appropriate.

**Academics:** Attendance, study time, grades, assignment updates.

**Skills and Career:** Practice time, project progress, milestone completion.

**Health and Fitness:** Workouts, sports, runs, and relevant measurements.

**Sleep and Recovery:** Sleep and wake times, optional quality ratings.

**Relationships and Social Life:** Commitments, visits, calls, and reminders.

**Personal Life and Recreation:** Reading, hobbies, creative activities, and personal projects.

**Custom Life Areas:** User-defined activities and measurements.

## 14.4 Derived Progress

The application should update related progress indicators from saved records where appropriate.

For example, completing a task should update its status and history. Completing a milestone should update its associated goal's progress.

Users should not have to enter the same information repeatedly.

However, time spent should not automatically be interpreted as proof that a goal has been achieved.

## 16.3 Three Progress Questions

**Where am I heading?**

# 17. LIFE SYNC

## 17.1 Purpose

Life Sync is the system's signature cross-domain intelligence experience.

It helps users understand how commitments, activities, deadlines, and priorities interact across life areas.

It should identify relevant conflicts, explain their consequences, and help users compare realistic alternatives.

## 17.2 Interface Direction

Life Sync should not be designed merely as a conventional chatbot window.

Explore an experience that communicates:

* The relevant commitments.
* The conflict or potential risk.
* Why the system identified it.
* Available scheduling alternatives.
* The likely consequences of each alternative.
* Actions the user may accept, modify, or reject.

The presentation should feel intelligent, contextual, and distinctive.

The Soul Orb may contribute to the visual identity, but ordinary controls and clearly labeled information must remain available.

## 17.3 Example

A student has an assignment due Saturday, a planned workout, and an evening event with friends.

Life Sync may present alternative arrangements for the assignment and workout while preserving important personal commitments where practical.

Recommendations must be grounded in recorded information, realistic estimated durations, deadlines, and user-defined priorities.

The system should explain relevant assumptions and distinguish confirmed scheduling conflicts from possible risks.

It must not silently change important plans or impose a universal hierarchy of life priorities.

Explore an interconnected, spatial, atmospheric representation.

## 18.3 Interaction

Potential capabilities include:

* Selecting goals and nodes.
* Revealing contextual information.
* Exploring connections.
* Filtering by life area.
* Filtering by status.
* Viewing active, paused, or completed items.

## 18.4 Visual Identity

Life Map may use more atmospheric depth and expressive visual treatment than data-dense areas such as Academics.

However, its graph must remain functional, navigable, and understandable.

# 19. AI ASSISTANT

## 19.1 Role

The AI assistant should serve as an accessible interface to Life Tracker's personal information.

It should help users ask questions, review their progress, make plans, and interact with supported application features through natural language.

It should not replace conventional navigation or prevent users from inspecting their underlying records.

## 19.2 Accessibility

Provide a persistent, easily discoverable entry point to the assistant.

Users should be able to open a fuller conversational interface when needed without allowing that interface to dominate every screen.

## 19.3 Data Grounding

The assistant must use actual application records.

It must not invent attendance percentages, grades, completed tasks, focus-session history, sleep data, deadlines, or goal progress.

When information is missing, it should acknowledge that limitation.

It must distinguish recorded facts, interpretations, assumptions, and recommendations.

## 19.4 Supported Interactions

Examples include:

* "What should I prioritize today?"
* "Log 45 minutes of machine-learning practice."
* "Help me reorganize tomorrow around my classes."
* "How is my attendance progressing?"
* "Which goals have received less attention than planned?"
* "Can I attend this event without jeopardizing my deadlines?"

Actions that modify consequential records should use clear, supported operations. Changes to schedules and other important information must remain under user control.

# 25. FINAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Life Tracker should help people stay connected to who they want to become while remaining aware of how they are actually living today.

Its design must express that philosophy.

The interface should be visually distinctive without being unnecessarily complicated, intelligent without being controlling, cinematic without compromising clarity, and motivating without turning life into a constant performance.

Life Orbit should make the user's life areas feel interconnected.

The Soul Orb should provide a recognizable visual identity.

The AI assistant should make personal information easier to understand and act upon.

Life Sync should help users understand trade-offs and make informed decisions.

Life Map should reveal the relationships among their ambitions, projects, and daily actions.

Individual life areas should feel personal and distinct while remaining parts of one coherent experience.

Above all, the interface should be built around meaningful functionality rather than decoration.

**The guiding principle is simple: create a warm, cinematic, intelligent personal environment that helps users bring order to life's complexity without losing sight of their health, relationships, ambitions, freedom, and enjoyment of life.**
